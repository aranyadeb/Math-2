## If this type comes, solve like this
Q: If a $\frac00$ limit contains unknown constants and its value is given
R: "$\lim_{x\to0}\frac{e^{(a-1)x}+2\cos bx+(c-2)e^{-x}}{x\cos x-\ln(1+x)}=2$", "$\lim\frac{1-\cos\alpha x\cos(\alpha+1)x\cos(\alpha+2)x}{\sin^2(\alpha+1)x}=2$", "$\lim_{x\to1}\frac{\sin(3x^2-4x+1)-x^2+1}{2x^3-7x^2+ax+b}=-2$".
F: e^x=1+x+\tfrac{x^2}2+\cdots,\ \cos x=1-\tfrac{x^2}2+\cdots,\ \ln(1+x)=x-\tfrac{x^2}2+\tfrac{x^3}3-\cdots,\ \sin x=x-\tfrac{x^3}6
S: Expand the denominator with series and find its first non-zero power, say $x^k$. || Expand the numerator up to $x^k$. || For a finite non-zero limit, all lower powers in the numerator must vanish: set those coefficients to 0. || The ratio of the $x^k$ coefficients equals the given value. || For $x\to a$, put $x=a+h$ first, or use that the denominator must vanish at $a$ (often a double root).
E: Denominator $\approx\frac{x^2}2$. Numerator: constant $c+1=0$, $x$-term $(a-1)-(c-2)=0$, $x^2$-term $3-b^2$. So $c=-1$, $a=-2$, $\frac{3-b^2}{1/2}=2\Rightarrow b^2=2$; $a^2+b^2+c^2=7$.
H: Trap: stopping the expansion too early — expand both sides to the same order.
P: 23:1,36,48,13

Q: If the limit is at a root of a quadratic ($x\to\beta$ where $\beta$ is a root)
R: "$\alpha,\beta$ roots of $x^2+bx+c=0$; $\lim_{x\to\beta}\frac{e^{2(x^2+bx+c)}-1-2(x^2+bx+c)}{(x-\beta)^2}$", "$\lim_{x\to\frac1\alpha}\left(\frac{1-\cos(x^2+bx+a)}{2(1-\alpha x)^2}\right)^{1/2}$".
F: x^2+bx+c=(x-\alpha)(x-\beta);\quad\frac{e^{u}-1-u}{u^2}\to\frac12,\ \ \frac{1-\cos u}{u^2}\to\frac12
S: Factor the quadratic using its roots: $x^2+bx+c=(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)$. || Let $u$ be the quadratic; use the standard small-$u$ limit. || Cancel $(x-\beta)^2$, leaving $(\beta-\alpha)^2$ times a constant. || For roots of $ax^2+bx+1$, note $\frac1\alpha,\frac1\beta$ are roots of $x^2+bx+a$.
E: $u=2(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)$: limit $=\frac12\cdot4(\beta-\alpha)^2=2(\alpha-\beta)^2$.
P: 23:16,26,29

Q: If the limit is of the form $1^\infty$
R: "$\lim(2-\cos x\sqrt{\cos2x})^{\frac{x+2}{x^2}}$", "$\lim(f(2+x))^{3/x}$ with $f(2)=1$", "$(\cos x)^{\cot x}$", "$\left(1+\frac{H_n}{n^2}\right)^n$", "$\left(\int_0^1(3x+5)^tdx\right)^{1/t}$".
F: \lim f^g=e^{\lim g\,(f-1)}\quad\text{when } f\to1,\ g\to\infty
S: Check that the base $\to1$ and the exponent $\to\infty$. || Write the answer as $e^{L}$ with $L=\lim g(x)\,(f(x)-1)$. || Evaluate $L$ with standard limits or series. || If the base is an integral, expand it to first order in $t$.
E: $1-\cos x\sqrt{\cos2x}\approx\frac{3x^2}2$, times $\frac{x+2}{x^2}\to3$: limit $e^3$, so $a=3$.
P: 23:3,15,17,25,39,41

Q: If the limit is $\frac{e-(1+x)^{1/x}}{x}$ type, or $(1^k+2^k+\cdots+n^k)^{1/k}$ with $k\to\infty$
R: "$\lim\frac{e-(1+2x)^{\frac1{2x}}}x$", "$\lim_{t\to0}\left(1^{\frac1{\sin^2t}}+\cdots+n^{\frac1{\sin^2t}}\right)^{\sin^2t}$".
F: (1+x)^{1/x}=e\left(1-\frac x2+\frac{11x^2}{24}-\cdots\right);\ \ (a_1^k+\cdots+a_n^k)^{1/k}\to\max a_i
S: Expand $(1+x)^{1/x}=e^{\frac{\ln(1+x)}x}=e^{1-\frac x2+\cdots}$. || Keep the first-order term. || For sums of powers, the largest term dominates.
E: $(1+2x)^{\frac1{2x}}\approx e(1-x)$, so the limit is $e$.

Q: If standard trig/exp/log limits combine (products and quotients near 0)
R: "$\frac{\tan(5x^{1/3})\ln(1+3x^2)}{(\tan^{-1}3\sqrt x)^2(e^{5x^{4/3}}-1)}$", "$\frac{\sin^2(\pi\cos^4x)}{x^4}$", "$\frac{\ln(\sec ex\cdot\sec e^2x\cdots)}{e^2-e^{2\cos x}}$", "$\frac{e^{\sqrt{\tan x}}-e^{\sqrt x}}{\sqrt{\tan x}-\sqrt x}$".
F: \sin u\sim u,\ \tan u\sim u,\ \tan^{-1}u\sim u,\ e^u-1\sim u,\ \ln(1+u)\sim u,\ \ln\sec u\sim\frac{u^2}2
S: Replace each factor by its simplest equivalent when its argument $\to0$. || For $\sin(\pi\cos^4x)$, use $\sin(\pi-\theta)=\sin\theta$: $\sin(\pi(1-\cos^4x))\approx\pi\cdot2x^2$. || Multiply/divide the equivalents and simplify. || For $\frac{e^a-e^b}{a-b}$ with $a,b\to0$ the limit is 1.
E: $\sin^2(\pi\cos^4x)\approx(2\pi x^2)^2$, so the limit is $4\pi^2$.

Q: If a product of cosines (or roots of cosines) appears in $1-\prod$
R: "$\lim\frac{2(1-\cos x\sqrt{\cos2x}\sqrt[3]{\cos3x}\cdots\sqrt[10]{\cos10x})}{x^2}$", "$\frac1{x^8}\left(1-\cos\frac{x^2}2-\cos\frac{x^2}4+\cos\frac{x^2}2\cos\frac{x^2}4\right)$".
F: 1-\prod(1-a_i)\approx\sum a_i\ (\text{small } a_i);\quad\sqrt[k]{\cos kx}\approx1-\frac{kx^2}2;\ \ 1-a-b+ab=(1-a)(1-b)
S: Write each factor as $1-a_i$ with $a_i$ small. || To first order, $1-\prod(1-a_i)=\sum a_i$. || If the expression factorises as $(1-\cos A)(1-\cos B)$, use $1-\cos u\approx\frac{u^2}2$ for each.
E: $\sum_{k=1}^{10}\frac{kx^2}2=\frac{55x^2}2$, so the limit is $55$.
P: 23:19,35

Q: If $\tan(\tan x)-\sin(\sin x)$ or $\tan^{-1}x+\ln\sqrt{\frac{1+x}{1-x}}-2x$ type differences are needed (higher-order series)
R: First-order terms cancel and you need $x^3$ or $x^5$ terms.
F: \tan x=x+\tfrac{x^3}3+\tfrac{2x^5}{15},\ \tan^{-1}x=x-\tfrac{x^3}3+\tfrac{x^5}5,\ \tanh^{-1}x=x+\tfrac{x^3}3+\tfrac{x^5}5
S: Expand both pieces to the order of the denominator. || Subtract; the leading terms cancel. || Divide by the denominator's leading term.
E: $\tan^{-1}x+\tanh^{-1}x-2x=\frac{2x^5}5+\cdots$, so the limit over $x^5$ is $\frac25$.
P: 23:12,20

Q: If the limit involves $\cos^{-1}$ or $\sin^{-1}$ at a non-zero point
R: "$\lim_{x\to\frac1{\sqrt2}}\frac{\sin(\cos^{-1}x)-x}{1-\tan(\cos^{-1}x)}$".
F: \text{put } \theta=\cos^{-1}x,\ x=\cos\theta
S: Substitute $\theta=\cos^{-1}x$, so $x=\cos\theta$ and $\theta\to\frac\pi4$. || Simplify: $\frac{\sin\theta-\cos\theta}{1-\tan\theta}=-\cos\theta$. || Put $\theta=\frac\pi4$.
E: Limit $=-\frac1{\sqrt2}$.

Q: If roots or radicals need rationalising (or $x\to\infty$ with $\sqrt{\ }$)
R: "$\frac{\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1+x^4}}-\sqrt2}{x^4}$", "$\lim_{x\to\infty}(\sqrt{x^2-x+1}-ax)=b$", "$\frac{(a+2x)^{1/3}-(3x)^{1/3}}{(3a+x)^{1/3}-(4x)^{1/3}}$", "$\csc x(\sqrt{2\cos^2x+3\cos x}-\sqrt{\cos^2x+\sin x+4})$".
F: \sqrt A-\sqrt B=\frac{A-B}{\sqrt A+\sqrt B};\ \ \sqrt{x^2+px+q}=x+\frac p2+O(\tfrac1x)
S: Multiply and divide by the conjugate (for cube roots, use $a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)$ or L'Hospital). || Simplify the new numerator; cancel the small factor. || At infinity, $a$ must make the leading terms cancel, and $b$ is the next term.
E: $\sqrt{x^2-x+1}=x-\frac12+\cdots$, so $a=1$, $b=-\frac12$.

Q: If $[x]$, $\{x\}$, $|x|$ or $\operatorname{sgn}$ appears (one-sided limits)
R: "$\frac{\tan(\pi\sin^2x)+(|x|-\sin(x[x]))^2}{x^2}$", "$\lim_{x\to7}\frac{18-[1-x]}{[x-3a]}$ exists", "$\frac{\cos^{-1}(1-\{x\}^2)\sin^{-1}(1-\{x\})}{\{x\}-\{x\}^3}$".
F: x\to0^+:\ [x]=0,\ \{x\}=x;\quad x\to0^-:\ [x]=-1,\ \{x\}=1+x
S: Compute the right-hand and left-hand limits separately. || Replace $[x]$, $\{x\}$, $|x|$ by their expressions on each side. || The limit exists only if both sides agree. || For $[\,]$ at a non-zero point, check the value just left and just right.
E: Right: $\pi+1$; left: $[x]=-1$ makes $|x|-\sin(-x)\approx0$, giving $\pi$. The limit does not exist.

Q: If $x\left([\frac1x]+[\frac2x]+\cdots\right)$ or $[x-5]-[2x+2]$ type expressions appear
R: "Least $p$ for which $\lim_{x\to0^+}\left(x([\frac1x]+\cdots+[\frac px])-x^2(\ldots)\right)\ge1$", "values of $a$ with $\lim_{x\to a}([x-5]-[2x+2])=0$".
F: x\left[\frac kx\right]\to k\ (x\to0^+);\ \ [x+n]=[x]+n
S: Use $\frac kx-1<[\frac kx]\le\frac kx$, so $x[\frac kx]\to k$ (and $x^2[\cdot]\to0$). || Sum to get $\frac{p(p+1)}2$ and solve the inequality. || For $[x-5]-[2x+2]$, write $=[x]-[2x]-7$ and check where it is constant near $a$.

Q: If a sum or a sum of powers is involved as $x\to1$ or $n\to\infty$
R: "$\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x+x^2+\cdots+x^n-n}{x-1}=820$", "$(nk+1)+\cdots+(nk+n)$ compared with $\sum r^k$", "$1+2-3+4+5-6+\cdots$ over a root expression", ratios with $(3x-1)^{x/2}$.
F: \lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^k-1}{x-1}=k;\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1^k+\cdots+n^k}{n^{k+1}}=\frac1{k+1}
S: Split into $\sum\frac{x^k-1}{x-1}$ and use $k$ for each; sum to $\frac{n(n+1)}2$. || For $n\to\infty$, keep only the highest powers of $n$ in numerator and denominator. || For exponentials like $(3x-1)^{x/2}$ against $\sqrt{(3x+2)^x}$, form the ratio and take a $1^\infty$ limit.
E: $\frac{n(n+1)}2=820\Rightarrow n=40$.

Q: If a series must be summed using $e$ (factorials)
R: "$\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{n^3(2n)!+(2n-1)n!}{n!(2n)!}=ae+\frac be+c$".
F: \sum\frac{n^3}{n!}=5e,\ \sum\frac{n^2}{n!}=2e,\ \sum\frac n{n!}=e;\ \ \sum\frac1{(2n)!}=\cosh1,\ \sum\frac1{(2n+1)!}=\sinh1
S: Split the general term into simple pieces. || Write $n^3=n(n-1)(n-2)+3n(n-1)+n$ so each piece is $\frac1{(n-k)!}$. || Odd/even factorial sums give $\cosh1$, $\sinh1$, and $\sinh1-\cosh1=-\frac1e$.
E: $\sum\frac{n^3}{n!}=5e$ and $\sum\frac{2n-1}{(2n)!}=-\frac1e$: $a=5$, $b=-1$, $c=0$; $a^2-b+c=26$.
P: 23:4

Q: If a limit of a telescoping $\tan^{-1}$ or $\tan$ series is needed
R: "$S_k=\sum\tan^{-1}\frac{6^r}{2^{2r+1}+3^{2r+1}}$", "$f(x)=\lim\sum\frac{\tan\frac x{2^{r+1}}+\tan^3\frac x{2^{r+1}}}{1-\tan^2\frac x{2^{r+1}}}$".
F: \tan^{-1}\frac{a-b}{1+ab}=\tan^{-1}a-\tan^{-1}b;\ \ \frac{\tan\theta+\tan^3\theta}{1-\tan^2\theta}=\tan2\theta-\tan\theta
S: Divide numerator and denominator to see the form $\frac{a-b}{1+ab}$ (here with $a=(\frac32)^{r+1}$, $b=(\frac32)^r$). || Telescope the sum. || Take $k\to\infty$: $\tan^{-1}(\infty)=\frac\pi2$.
E: $S_\infty=\frac\pi2-\tan^{-1}\frac32=\tan^{-1}\frac23$.
