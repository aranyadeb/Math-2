Q: If $y$ is an inverse-trig function of a messy expression (simplify before differentiating)
R: "$2y=\left(\cot^{-1}\frac{\sqrt3\cos x+\sin x}{\cos x-\sqrt3\sin x}\right)^2$", "$\sum k\cos^{-1}\{\frac35\cos kx-\frac45\sin kx\}$", "$\sin(\cos^{-1}\frac{1-2^{2x}}{1+2^{2x}})$", "$\tan^{-1}(\sec x^3-\tan x^3)$".
F: \frac{\sqrt3+\tan x}{1-\sqrt3\tan x}=\tan\left(x+\frac\pi3\right);\ \ a\cos\theta-b\sin\theta=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\cos(\theta+\alpha);\ \ \cos^{-1}\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}=2\tan^{-1}t
S: Recognise the standard substitution: divide by $\cos x$ to get $\tan(x\pm\alpha)$; write $\frac35,\frac45$ as $\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha$; put $t=2^x=\tan\theta$. || Simplify the inverse-trig of a trig function, taking care that the angle is in the principal range for the given $x$. || Now $y$ is a simple function of $x$; differentiate.
E: $\cos^{-1}(\cos(kx+\alpha))=kx+\alpha$ near $0$, so $y=\sum k(kx+\alpha)$ and $y'(0)=\sum_{k=1}^6k^2=91$.
H: Trap: $\cot^{-1}(\tan\theta)=\frac\pi2-\theta$ only for $\theta\in(-\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2)$; outside, add $\pi$.

Q: If one function is differentiated with respect to another function
R: "Derivative of $\tan^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{1+x^2}-1}x$ with respect to $\tan^{-1}\frac{2x\sqrt{1-x^2}}{1-2x^2}$ at $x=\frac12$".
F: \frac{du}{dv}=\frac{du/dx}{dv/dx};\ \ \tan^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{1+x^2}-1}x=\tfrac12\tan^{-1}x
S: Simplify both $u$ and $v$ by substitution ($x=\tan\theta$, $x=\sin\theta$). || Differentiate each with respect to $x$. || Divide and substitute the point.
E: $u=\frac12\tan^{-1}x$, $v=2\sin^{-1}x$ ⇒ $\frac{du}{dv}=\frac{\sqrt{1-x^2}}{4(1+x^2)}=\frac{\sqrt3}{10}$ at $x=\frac12$.

Q: If the curve is $y\sqrt{1-x^2}+x\sqrt{1-y^2}=k$ (or another symmetric implicit form)
R: "$y\sqrt{1-x^2}=k-x\sqrt{1-y^2}$, $y(\frac12)=-\frac14$; $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $x=\frac12$".
F: x=\sin A,\ y=\sin B\Rightarrow\sin(A+B)=k\Rightarrow\sin^{-1}x+\sin^{-1}y=c
S: Substitute $x=\sin A$, $y=\sin B$: the left side is $\sin(A+B)$. || So $\sin^{-1}x+\sin^{-1}y$ is constant. || Differentiate: $\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}+\frac{y'}{\sqrt{1-y^2}}=0$.
E: $y'=-\frac{\sqrt{1-y^2}}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}=-\frac{\sqrt{15}/4}{\sqrt3/2}=-\frac{\sqrt5}2$.

Q: If an implicit curve is given and $y'$ or $y''$ at a point is asked
R: "$(x^2+y^2-3)+(x^2-y^2-1)^5=0$ at $(\alpha,\alpha)$; $3y'-y^3y''$".
F: \frac{d}{dx}F(x,y)=F_x+F_y\,y'=0
S: Find the point first (put $y=x$ in the curve). || Differentiate the whole equation with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function. || Put the point to get $y'$. || Differentiate again for $y''$ and substitute $x$, $y$, $y'$.
E: At $(\alpha,\alpha)$ the second bracket is $(-1)^5$, so $2\alpha^2-4=0$, $\alpha=\sqrt2$; differentiating gives $y'=-\frac xy$ there.

Q: If $y=u^v$ (variable base and exponent) or $x^y$ and $y^x$ appear
R: "$2x^y+3y^x=20$; $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $(2,2)$", "$y=(x^x)^x$; $\frac{d^2x}{dy^2}$ at $x=1$", "$\log_{\cos x}\csc x$".
F: \frac d{dx}u^v=u^v\left(v'\ln u+\frac{vu'}u\right),\ \ \log_ab=\frac{\ln b}{\ln a}
S: Take logs (or use the $u^v$ formula). || For implicit $x^y$, $y^x$: differentiate each term with the formula, then substitute the point and solve for $y'$. || Change log bases to natural logs before differentiating.
E: At $(2,2)$: $8(y'\ln2+1)+12(\ln2+y')=0\Rightarrow y'=-\frac{2+3\ln2}{3+2\ln2}$.

Q: If $\frac{d^2x}{dy^2}$, or a parametric second derivative, is asked
R: "$y=(x^x)^x$; $\frac{d^2x}{dy^2}+20$ at $x=1$", "$x=3\tan t$, $y=3\sec t$; $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ at $t=\frac\pi4$".
F: \frac{d^2x}{dy^2}=-\frac{y''}{(y')^3};\qquad\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)\Big/\frac{dx}{dt}
S: For $\frac{d^2x}{dy^2}$, find $y'$ and $y''$ with respect to $x$, then use the formula (not the reciprocal of $y''$). || For parametric curves, find $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y_t}{x_t}$, differentiate that with respect to $t$, then divide by $x_t$ again.
E: $x=3\tan t$, $y=3\sec t$: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\sin t$, $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\frac{\cos t}{3\sec^2t}=\frac{\cos^3t}3=\frac1{6\sqrt2}$ at $\frac\pi4$.

Q: If a product of many factors is differentiated at a point where one factor vanishes
R: "$y=(1+x)(1+x^2)(1+x^4)(1+x^8)(1+x^{16})$; $y'-y''$ at $x=-1$".
F: y=(1+x)g(x)\Rightarrow y'(-1)=g(-1),\ y''(-1)=2g'(-1)
S: Separate the factor that vanishes at the point. || Use the product rule: terms containing that factor vanish. || Compute $g(-1)$ and $g'(-1)$ (log differentiation for $g'$).
E: $g(-1)=16$, $g'(-1)=-8(2+4+8+16)=-240$ ⇒ $y'-y''=16+480=496$.

Q: If $f(x)=x^3+x^2f'(1)+xf''(2)+f'''(3)$ type (derivative values appear as coefficients)
R: Coefficients of a polynomial are its own derivatives at given points.
F: \text{put } a=f'(1),\ b=f''(2),\ c=f'''(3)
S: Write $f=x^3+ax^2+bx+c$. || Differentiate: $f'=3x^2+2ax+b$, $f''=6x+2a$, $f'''=6$. || Equate $c=6$, $b=12+2a$, $a=3+2a+b$; solve. || Evaluate what is asked.
E: $a=-5$, $b=2$, $c=6$: $f(2)=-2$, and $f'(10)=300-100+2=202$.

Q: If a functional equation is used to find $f$ and then derivative values
R: "$f(x+y)=2^xf(y)+4^yf(x)$, $f(2)=3$", "$f'(1)=\lim a^2f(\frac1a)$", "$f+f'+f''=x^5+64$", "$f(\frac{x+y}3)=\frac{f(x)+f(y)}3$, $f'(0)=3$".
F: \text{swap } x \leftrightarrow y \text{ and compare};\ \ f+f'+f''=P\Rightarrow f=P-P'+P'''-P''''+\cdots
S: Use symmetry: swap $x$ and $y$ and equate the two forms, or put $y=0$. || Get the closed form of $f$ (e.g. $k(4^x-2^x)$, or a linear function). || For $f+f'+f''=P$ (polynomial), assume $f$ is a polynomial of the same degree and compare, or use the operator series. || Differentiate the closed form.
E: $f(x)=\frac14(4^x-2^x)$; $\frac{f'(4)}{f'(2)}=\frac{2\cdot256-16}{2\cdot16-4}=\frac{124}7$; answer $248$.

Q: If $y$ is given implicitly and a differential equation like $(x^2-1)y''+\alpha xy'+\beta y=0$ must be verified
R: "$y^{1/4}+y^{-1/4}=2x$", "$y=\sin(m\sin^{-1}x)$" type questions asking $\alpha$, $\beta$.
F: y^{1/4}=x\pm\sqrt{x^2-1}\Rightarrow\frac{y'}y=\frac4{\sqrt{x^2-1}}
S: Solve for $y$ (or $\ln y$) in a simple form. || Differentiate once and clear the square root: $\sqrt{x^2-1}\,y'=4y$. || Square and differentiate again (or differentiate directly) to get the second-order equation. || Compare coefficients.
E: $(x^2-1)y''+xy'-16y=0$: $\alpha=1$, $\beta=-16$, $|\alpha-\beta|=17$.

E: Expanding gives $y=-\sin x+\ldots$ type terms plus a constant; $y''+y$ equals that constant.

Q: If the chain rule is applied through nested functions at a point
R: "$\sin^3\left(\frac\pi3\cos\left(\frac\pi{3\sqrt2}(-4x^3+5x^2+1)^{3/2}\right)\right)$ at $x=1$", "$(f\circ g)'(\alpha)$ with $f=\ln\sin x$, $g=\sin^{-1}e^{-x}$", "$f(\frac{7\pi}{12})f''(\frac{7\pi}{12})$".
F: \frac{d}{dx}f(g(h(x)))=f'(g(h))\cdot g'(h)\cdot h'(x)
S: Work from the inside out: evaluate the innermost function at the point, then the next, etc. || Multiply the derivatives layer by layer. || Simplify compositions first if possible: $\ln\sin(\sin^{-1}e^{-x})=-x$.
E: $f(g(x))=\ln e^{-x}=-x$, so $(f\circ g)'=-1$ and $(f\circ g)(\alpha)=-\alpha$.

Q: If a piecewise function must be differentiable and constants found (or $P(x)/\sin(x-2)$ continuous)
R: "$3x^2+k\sqrt{x+1}$ for $0<x<1$, $mx^2+k^2$ for $x\ge1$, differentiable", "$f=\frac{P(x)}{\sin(x-2)}$, $f(2)=7$, $P''$ constant, $P(3)=9$".
F: \text{continuity: values equal};\ \text{differentiability: derivatives equal}
S: Equate the two pieces at the joining point. || Equate their derivatives at that point. || Solve for the constants. || For $\frac{P(x)}{\sin(x-2)}$ continuous at 2: $P(2)=0$ and $P'(2)=7$; with $P$ quadratic, use $P(3)$ to finish.
E: $3+k\sqrt2=m+k^2$ and $6+\frac k{2\sqrt2}=2m$.
