Q: If the length of a focal chord or its distance from the vertex is involved
R: "Focal chord of $y^2=12x$ of length 15; distance from the origin $p$", "focal chord of length $l$ and slope $m$; $ld^2$", "focal chord of $y^2=36x$ of length 100".
F: y^2=4ax:\ \text{focal chord at angle }\theta:\ L=4a\csc^2\theta,\ \ \text{distance from vertex}=a\sin\theta
S: Let the chord make angle $\theta$ with the axis (slope $m=\tan\theta$). || Length $L=4a\csc^2\theta=\frac{4a(1+m^2)}{m^2}$; solve for $\sin^2\theta$ or $m$. || The perpendicular distance from the vertex to the chord is $a\sin\theta$. || Endpoints: parameters $t$ and $-\frac1t$ with $t-\frac1t=\frac2m$.
E: $y^2=12x$ ($a=3$): $12\csc^2\theta=15\Rightarrow\sin^2\theta=\frac45$; $p^2=9\cdot\frac45$, $10p^2=72$.
H: Note $ld^2=4a\csc^2\theta\cdot a^2\sin^2\theta=4a^3$ — independent of the slope.

Q: If focal distances $SP$, $SQ$ of a focal chord, or feet of perpendiculars on the directrix, are used
R: "$(SP)(SQ)=\frac{147}4$; circle on $PQ$ as diameter", "$M,N$ feet of perpendiculars from $P,Q$ on the directrix".
F: \frac1{SP}+\frac1{SQ}=\frac1a,\quad SP=a(1+t^2),\quad\text{circle on a focal chord as diameter touches the directrix}
S: Write $SP=a+x_P$ (distance to directrix). || Use the harmonic relation $\frac1{SP}+\frac1{SQ}=\frac1a$, so $PQ=SP+SQ=\frac{SP\cdot SQ}a$. || $PM=SP$ and $QN=SQ$ (focus–directrix property); $\angle MSN=90^\circ$.
E: $a=3$: $PQ=\frac{147/4}3=\frac{49}4$, so the circle has radius $\frac{49}8$.

Q: If a line cuts the parabola and a triangle with the focus (centroid, area) is given
R: "$y=mx+c$ meets $y^2=8x$ at $B,C$; centroid of $ABC$ with focus $A$ is $(\frac73,\frac43)$".
F: my^2-4ay+4ac=0\Rightarrow y_1+y_2=\frac{4a}m,\ y_1y_2=\frac{4ac}m
S: Eliminate $x$: substitute $x=\frac{y-c}m$ into $y^2=4ax$. || Read $y_1+y_2$ and $y_1y_2$ (Vieta). || Centroid: $\frac{y_1+y_2+0}3$ and $\frac{x_1+x_2+a}3$ with $x_i=\frac{y_i-c}m$. || Solve for $m$ and $c$.
E: $y_1+y_2=\frac8m=4\Rightarrow m=2$; $x_1+x_2=5\Rightarrow\frac{4-2c}2=5\Rightarrow c=-3$.

Q: If the point of the parabola nearest to a point or a line is asked
R: "Point of $x=4y^2$ nearest to $(0,33)$", "shortest distance between $y=x$ and $y^2=x-2$", "min distance between a circle and $y=x^2$".
F: \text{nearest to a line: tangent parallel to the line};\ \ \text{nearest to a point: normal passes through the point}
S: For a line: set the slope of the tangent equal to the line's slope; find the point; distance from it to the line. || For a point: the normal at the nearest point passes through the given point (or minimise the squared distance). || For a circle: the normal of the parabola passes through the circle's centre; subtract the radius.
E: $y^2=x-2$: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac1{2y}=1\Rightarrow(\frac94,\frac12)$; distance $\frac{7/4}{\sqrt2}=\frac7{4\sqrt2}$.

Q: If the image of a parabola in a line is needed
R: "Image of $x^2=4y$ in $x-y=1$ is $(y+a)^2=b(x-c)$", "mirror image of $y^2=4x$ in $x+y+4=0$ meets $y+5=0$".
F: \text{in } x-y=k:\ (x,y)\to(y+k,\ x-k);\ \ \text{in } x+y=k:\ (x,y)\to(k-y,\ k-x)
S: Write the image map for the mirror line (for slopes $\pm1$ it is a swap with shift). || If $(X,Y)$ is on the image, its mirror $(x,y)$ is on the original: express $x,y$ in $X,Y$ and substitute. || Compare with the required form.
E: $x=Y+1$, $y=X-1$: $(Y+1)^2=4(X-1)$ ⇒ $a=1,b=4,c=1$.
