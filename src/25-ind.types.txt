Q: If the numerator is a combination of the denominator and its derivative ($\int\frac{ae^x+be^{-x}}{ce^x+de^{-x}}$, $\frac{a\sin x+b\cos x}{c\sin x+d\cos x}$)
R: "$\int\frac{2e^x+3e^{-x}}{4e^x+7e^{-x}}dx=\frac1{14}(ux+v\ln(4e^x+7e^{-x}))$".
F: \text{Num}=A\cdot\text{Den}+B\cdot\text{Den}'\ \Rightarrow\ \int=Ax+B\ln|\text{Den}|
S: Write the numerator as $A(\text{denominator})+B(\text{derivative of denominator})$. || Compare coefficients of $e^x$ and $e^{-x}$ (or $\sin$, $\cos$) to get $A$, $B$. || The integral is $Ax+B\ln|\text{Den}|+C$. || Match with the given form.
E: $4A+4B=2$, $7A-7B=3$ ⇒ $A=\frac{13}{28}$, $B=\frac1{28}$, so $u=\frac{13}2$, $v=\frac12$, $u+v=7$.

Q: If the integrand has $\frac{x^2\pm1}{x^4+kx^2+1}$ or $\tan^{-1}\frac{x^2+1}x$ (divide by $x^2$)
R: "$\int\frac{(x^2-1)+\tan^{-1}\frac{x^2+1}x}{(x^4+3x^2+1)\tan^{-1}\frac{x^2+1}x}dx$".
F: \frac{x^2-1}{x^4+kx^2+1}=\frac{1-\frac1{x^2}}{(x+\frac1x)^2+k-2},\quad\frac{x^2+1}{x^4+kx^2+1}=\frac{1+\frac1{x^2}}{(x-\frac1x)^2+k+2}
S: Split the integrand into simpler fractions. || Divide numerator and denominator by $x^2$. || For $x^2-1$ put $t=x+\frac1x$; for $x^2+1$ put $t=x-\frac1x$. || Note $\frac d{dx}\tan^{-1}(x+\frac1x)=\frac{x^2-1}{x^4+3x^2+1}$, so the first piece is $\int\frac{dT}T=\ln T$. || Write $1=\frac12[(x^2+1)-(x^2-1)]$ to split a plain $\frac1{x^4+kx^2+1}$.
E: First piece: $\ln\left(\tan^{-1}\frac{x^2+1}x\right)$; the remaining $\int\frac{dx}{x^4+3x^2+1}$ splits into $\frac1{2\sqrt5}\tan^{-1}\frac{x-1/x}{\sqrt5}-\frac12\tan^{-1}(x+\frac1x)$.

Q: If $\sin$ and $\cos$ appear with powers whose sum is a negative even integer (or $\sin^3+\cos^3$ in the denominator)
R: "$\int\sin^{-11/2}x\cos^{-5/2}x\,dx$", "$\int\frac{\sin^{3/2}x+\cos^{3/2}x}{\sqrt{\sin^3x\cos^3x\sin(x-\theta)}}$", "$\int\frac{\sin x}{\sin^3x+\cos^3x}dx$", "$\int\frac{d\theta}{\cos^2\theta(\tan2\theta+\sec2\theta)}$".
F: \sin^mx\cos^nx,\ m+n=-2k:\ \text{divide by } \cos^{2k}x,\ \text{put } t=\tan x
S: Divide numerator and denominator by $\cos^{|m+n|}x$ so everything is in $\tan x$ and $\sec^2x$. || Put $t=\tan x$, $dt=\sec^2xdx$; use $\sec^2x=1+t^2$ for leftover powers. || Integrate the resulting rational/power function (partial fractions for $\frac t{t^3+1}$). || Return to $\tan x$ (or $\cot x$).
E: $\cos^2\theta(\tan2\theta+\sec2\theta)=\cos^2\theta\frac{1+\tan\theta}{1-\tan\theta}$, so $\int\frac{1-t}{1+t}dt=-t+2\ln|1+t|$: $(\lambda,f)=(-1,1+\tan\theta)$.

Q: If the integrand is $\frac{\cos x}{\sin^3x(1+\sin^6x)^{2/3}}$ type (take out the highest power)
R: A power of $(1+t^n)$ times a power of $t$, after $t=\sin x$.
F: \int\frac{dt}{t^{n+1}(1+t^n)^{p}}:\ \text{write } (1+t^n)=t^n(1+t^{-n}),\ u=1+t^{-n}
S: Put $t=\sin x$. || Take the highest power of $t$ out of the bracket. || Substitute $u=1+t^{-n}$; $du=-nt^{-n-1}dt$. || Integrate the power of $u$ and return.
E: $-\frac16\int u^{-2/3}du=-\frac12(1+\sin^{-6}x)^{1/3}=-\frac{(1+\sin^6x)^{1/3}}{2\sin^2x}$: $\lambda=3$, $\lambda f(\frac\pi3)=-2$.

Q: If it is $\int\frac{dx}{(x-a)^p(x-b)^q}$ with $p+q=2$
R: "$\int\frac{dx}{(x-11)^{11/13}(x+15)^{15/13}}$".
F: t=\frac{x-a}{x-b},\quad dt=\frac{a-b}{(x-b)^2}\cdot(-1)\,dx\ \ \left(\frac{b-a}{(x-b)^2}dx\right)
S: Divide inside to write the integrand as $\frac1{(x-b)^2}\left(\frac{x-a}{x-b}\right)^{-p}$. || Put $t=\frac{x-a}{x-b}$. || Integrate the power of $t$.
E: $dt=\frac{26}{(x+15)^2}dx$: $\frac1{26}\int t^{-11/13}dt=\frac14t^{2/13}$.

Q: If two different roots of $x$ appear ($x^{2/3}$ and $x^{1/2}$)
R: "$\int\frac{dx}{x^{2/3}+2x^{1/2}}$, $f(0)=-26+24\ln2$".
F: x=t^{\operatorname{lcm}},\ \text{here } t=x^{1/6}
S: Put $x=t^L$ with $L$ the lcm of the root orders. || All powers become integers; simplify. || Divide polynomials and integrate. || Use the given value to fix $C$.
E: $6\int\frac{t^2}{t+2}dt=3t^2-12t+24\ln(t+2)+C$; $C=-26$; $f(1)=-35+24\ln3$, so $a+b=-11$.

Q: If the integrand is $e^x[f+f']$ or $[xf'+f]$ in disguise
R: "$\int\left(\frac x{x\sin x+\cos x}\right)^2dx$", "$\int\frac{1-\sin(\ln t)}{1-\cos(\ln t)}dt$", "$\int\left((\frac xe)^{2x}+(\frac ex)^{2x}\right)\ln x\,dx$".
F: \int e^x[f(x)+f'(x)]dx=e^xf(x)+C,\ \ \frac d{dx}\left(\frac xe\right)^{2x}=2\ln x\left(\frac xe\right)^{2x}
S: Look for a substitution that produces $e^u$ (e.g. $t=e^u$ when $\ln t$ appears). || Split the remaining factor into $f+f'$: for half angles, $\frac{1-\sin u}{1-\cos u}=\frac12\csc^2\frac u2-\cot\frac u2$. || Read off $e^uf(u)$. || For $(\frac xe)^{2x}$, differentiate it directly — it already gives $\ln x$.
E: $\int e^u\left(\frac12\csc^2\frac u2-\cot\frac u2\right)du=-e^u\cot\frac u2$; with $f(e^{\pi/2})=-e^{\pi/2}$, $\alpha=-\cot\frac\pi8=-(1+\sqrt2)$.

Q: If it is $\int x^n\sin x$ or $\int x^ne^x$ (repeated parts)
R: "$\int x^3\sin x\,dx=g(x)+C$; $8(g(\frac\pi2)+g'(\frac\pi2))$".
F: \int x^3\sin x=-x^3\cos x+3x^2\sin x+6x\cos x-6\sin x;\quad g'=\text{integrand}
S: Use the tabular (DI) method: differentiate $x^n$ down to 0, integrate $\sin x$ repeatedly, alternate signs. || Note $g'(x)$ is just the integrand. || Substitute the point.
E: $g(\frac\pi2)=\frac{3\pi^2}4-6$, $g'(\frac\pi2)=\frac{\pi^3}8$: $8(\ldots)=\pi^3+6\pi^2-48$, $\alpha+\beta-\gamma=55$.

Q: If it is $\int\frac{dx}{(ax^2+b)\sqrt{cx^2+d}}$ or $\frac1x\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{1+x}}$
R: "$\int\frac{dx}{(3+4x^2)\sqrt{4-3x^2}}$", "$\int\frac1x\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{1+x}}dx$, $g(1)=0$".
F: \text{put } x=\frac1t\ \text{(then } u=\sqrt{ct^2... }\text{)};\quad\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{1+x}}=\frac{1-x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}
S: For $(ax^2+b)\sqrt{cx^2+d}$: put $x=\frac1t$, then $u^2=dt^2+c$ — it reduces to $\int\frac{du}{\text{quadratic}}$. || For $\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{1+x}}$: rationalise to $\frac{1-x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$, split into $\int\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{1-x^2}}-\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$. || Use the given value to fix the constant.

Q: If $\int\frac{dx}{(x^2+x+1)^2}$ (repeated quadratic factor) appears
R: "$\int\frac{dx}{(x^2+x+1)^2}=a\tan^{-1}\frac{2x+1}{\sqrt3}+b\frac{2x+1}{x^2+x+1}$".

Q: If the integrand has powers of $\sqrt{1+x^2}+x$
R: "$\int\frac{(\sqrt{1+x^2}+x)^{10}}{(\sqrt{1+x^2}-x)^9}dx$".
F: t=\sqrt{1+x^2}+x,\ \ \frac1t=\sqrt{1+x^2}-x,\ \ dx=\frac12\left(1+\frac1{t^2}\right)dt
S: Put $t=\sqrt{1+x^2}+x$; then $\sqrt{1+x^2}-x=\frac1t$, so the integrand is $t^{19}$. || $x=\frac12(t-\frac1t)$ gives $dx$. || Integrate the powers of $t$ and rewrite.
E: $\frac12\int(t^{19}+t^{17})dt=\frac{t^{20}}{40}+\frac{t^{18}}{36}$.

Q: If the integrand is the derivative of a quotient (high powers over a squared polynomial)
R: "$\int\frac{7x^{10}+9x^8}{(1+x^2+2x^9)^2}dx$".
F: \int\frac{-u'}{u^2}dx=\frac1u
S: Divide numerator and denominator by the power that makes the denominator $(\ldots+\text{const})^2$ (here $x^{18}$). || The numerator becomes minus the derivative of the new bracket. || Answer $\frac1u$, then simplify back.
E: $u=x^{-9}+x^{-7}+2$, integral $\frac1u=\frac{x^9}{1+x^2+2x^9}$; $f(1)=\frac14$ ✓.
