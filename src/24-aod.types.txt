Q: If it asks where $f$ is increasing or decreasing (sign of $f'$)
R: "$(2,3)$ is the largest interval where $2\ln(x-2)-x^2+ax+1$ increases", "$(3x-7)x^{2/3}$ is increasing for…", "$x^x$ increases on…", "$xe^{x(1-x)}$", "$3\ln|\frac{x-1}{x+1}|-\frac2{x-1}$".
F: f'(x)>0\Rightarrow\text{increasing},\ \ f'(x)<0\Rightarrow\text{decreasing}
S: Write the domain. || Differentiate and factor $f'(x)$ completely (including denominators and points where $f'$ does not exist). || Mark zeros and undefined points on a number line; find the sign in each interval (wavy curve). || Read the intervals; an endpoint of a "largest interval" is a zero of $f'$, which fixes a parameter.
E: $f'(x)=\frac{15x-14}{3x^{1/3}}>0$ for $x<0$ or $x>\frac{14}{15}$.

Q: If the function is piecewise, composite or built from a functional equation, and its monotonic intervals are asked
R: "$f$ given in three pieces; set where it increases", "$5f(x)+4f(\frac1x)=x^2-2$, $y=9x^2f(x)$", "$x\cos^{-1}(-\sin|x|)$", "$h=f\circ g$ with $f=e^x-x$, $g=x^2-x$", "$e^{x^3-3x+1}$ one-one onto".
F: (f\circ g)'=f'(g(x))\,g'(x)
S: Find the explicit formula (solve the functional equation, or write each piece). || Differentiate each piece and study the sign on its own interval; check the joining points. || For composites, the sign of $f'(g(x))g'(x)$ is the product of two signs. || For one-one onto, the function must be monotonic on the domain; its range gives $(a,b]$.
E: $h'(x)=(e^{x^2-x}-1)(2x-1)$: positive when both factors have the same sign ⇒ $x\in[0,\frac12]\cup[1,\infty)$.

Q: If the number of real roots of an equation is asked

F: \text{cubic } f \text{ has 3 real roots}\iff f(x_{\max})\cdot f(x_{\min})<0

E: $5x^3-15x$ has local max $10$ at $x=-1$, min $-10$ at $x=1$; three roots iff $a\in(-10,10)$; $\beta-2\alpha=30$.

Q: If it is a rate-of-change problem (melting coat, filling cone, expanding cube or balloon)
R: "Ice of uniform thickness melts at $50\text{ cm}^3/\text{min}$", "water into a cone at $1\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$", "surface area of a cube increasing at $3.6$", "balloon's surface area increases at a constant rate".
F: \frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{dV}{dr}\cdot\frac{dr}{dt};\ \ \text{cone with } \frac rh=k:\ V=\tfrac13\pi k^2h^3
S: Write the quantity (volume/area) in terms of one variable only (use similar triangles for cones). || Differentiate with respect to $t$ by the chain rule. || Substitute the given rate and the instant's value. || Solve for the unknown rate.
E: Cube: $\frac{dS}{dt}=12a\frac{da}{dt}=3.6$, so $\frac{dV}{dt}=3a^2\frac{da}{dt}=0.9a=9$ at $a=10$.

Q: If $g(x)=f(\phi(x))+\ldots$ with $f''>0$ and it asks where $g$ increases or decreases
R: "$g(x)=3f(\frac x3)+f(3-x)$, $f''>0$ on $(0,3)$", "$g(x)=f(\tan^2x-2\tan x+4a)$", "for which $a$ is $f(g(x))$ increasing".
F: f''>0\Rightarrow f'\ \text{increasing}\Rightarrow f'(u)>f'(v)\iff u>v
S: Differentiate $g$; the result compares $f'$ at two arguments. || Since $f'$ is increasing, $f'(u)-f'(v)$ has the sign of $u-v$. || Solve the simple inequality between the arguments.
E: $g'(x)=f'(\frac x3)-f'(3-x)<0\iff\frac x3<3-x\iff x<\frac94$; $8\alpha=18$.

Q: If a parameter must make $f$ increasing for all $x$

E: $D\le0\Rightarrow\lambda\le\frac13$; with $\lambda=\frac13$: $f(1)+f(-1)=72$.

Q: If Rolle's theorem, MVT or convexity is used to prove or count something
R: "$f(0)=3$, $f(1)=5$, line $y=2x+3$ meets the graph at only two points", "$f(2)=f(4)=0$: there exist $x_1,x_2$ with $f'(x_1)=-1$…", "$fm^2-2f'm+f''=0$ has equal roots", "$\sin x+3x-\frac2\pi(x^2+x)$".
F: \text{MVT: } f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a};\ \ \text{Rolle: } f(a)=f(b)\Rightarrow f'(c)=0
S: Identify an interval where the hypotheses hold. || Apply MVT/Rolle to get points where $f'$ takes given values. || Apply again to $f'$ to get information on $f''$. || Equal roots in $m$ means $(f')^2=ff''$, i.e. $\left(\frac{f'}f\right)'=0$: $f=Ae^{kx}$.

Q: If an inequality or a comparison of function values is asked via monotonicity
R: "$\frac x{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}}-\frac{d-x}{\sqrt{b^2+(d-x)^2}}$ is increasing?", "$|\ln x|-|x-1|$: which statements are true", "$(2-\sin\frac1x)|x|$", "$\lim\frac{f(5x)}{f(x)}$ with $f$ increasing and $\frac{f(7x)}{f(x)}\to1$".

S: Differentiate and show $f'>0$ (or study pieces separately for moduli). || Use monotonicity to order values. || For limit comparisons, trap $\frac{f(5x)}{f(x)}$ between $1$ and $\frac{f(7x)}{f(x)}$.
E: $1\le\frac{f(5x)}{f(x)}\le\frac{f(7x)}{f(x)}\to1$, so the limit of $\frac{f(5x)}{f(x)}-1$ is 0.

Q: If critical points of a polynomial (or log/power function) are given and its coefficients or extreme values are asked

F: f'(c)=0\ \text{at each critical point};\ \ f'\ \text{has the factors } (x-c_i)
S: Write $f'(x)$ with unknown coefficients and set $f'(c_i)=0$ (or write $f'=k\prod(x-c_i)$ for polynomials). || Use the extra condition (a value, a limit, touching an axis) to fix the remaining constants. || Integrate $f'$ if needed. || Then compute max/min values.
E: Degree 4 with critical points $-1,0,1$: $f'=kx(x^2-1)$, so $f=\frac k4x^4-\frac k2x^2+c$; $f(x)=f(0)$ ⇒ $x^2=0,2$, sum of squares $4$.
