Q: If the limits are symmetric ($-a$ to $a$) and the integrand looks ugly ($1+e^{\sin x}$, $1+\pi^x$, odd parts)
R: "$\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{8\sqrt2\cos x}{(1+e^{\sin x})(1+\sin^4x)}dx$", "$\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\left(\frac{x^2\cos x}{1+\pi^x}+\frac{1+\sin^2x}{1+e^{\sin x^{2023}}}\right)dx$", "$\int_{-1}^1\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+1})dx$".
F: \int_{-a}^af(x)dx=\int_0^a[f(x)+f(-x)]dx;\quad\frac1{1+e^{g}}+\frac1{1+e^{-g}}=1
S: Replace the integral by $\int_0^a[f(x)+f(-x)]dx$. || Terms like $\frac1{1+e^{\text{odd}}}$ or $\frac1{1+\pi^x}$ pair up to 1. || Odd functions integrate to 0; even functions double. || Finish the simpler integral (often with $t=\sin x$).
E: Pairing gives $\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{8\sqrt2\cos x}{1+\sin^4x}dx=8\sqrt2\int_0^1\frac{dt}{1+t^4}=2\pi+2\ln(3+2\sqrt2)$; $\alpha^2+\beta^2=8$.

Q: If the limits are not symmetric but the integrand has an odd part ($\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+1})$ from $-1$ to $2$)
R: "$\int_{-1}^2\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+1})dx$".
F: \int_{-1}^2=\int_{-1}^1+\int_1^2,\ \ \int\sinh^{-1}x\,dx=x\sinh^{-1}x-\sqrt{x^2+1}
S: Split off the symmetric part; the odd part gives 0 there. || Integrate the remainder by parts.
E: $\left[x\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+1})-\sqrt{x^2+1}\right]_1^2=2\ln(2+\sqrt5)-\sqrt5-\ln(1+\sqrt2)+\sqrt2$.
P: 14:14

Q: If the integrand is $\frac{f(x)}{f(x)+f(a+b-x)}$, or $x$ times a function symmetric about the midpoint
R: "$I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^{3/2}x}{\sin^{3/2}x+\cos^{3/2}x}dx$; then $\int_0^{2I}\frac{x\sin x\cos x}{\sin^4x+\cos^4x}dx$", "$\int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/4}\frac{dx}{\sin2x(\tan^5x+\cot^5x)}$".
F: \int_a^bf(x)dx=\int_a^bf(a+b-x)dx;\quad\int_0^a xg(x)dx=\frac a2\int_0^ag(x)dx\ \text{if } g(a-x)=g(x)
S: Apply $x\to a+b-x$ and add to the original: the denominator cancels and you get $\frac{b-a}2$. || If $x$ multiplies a function symmetric about the midpoint, replace $x$ by $\frac a2$. || For $\tan^n+\cot^n$, put $t=\tan x$ and then $u=t^n$ type substitutions.
E: $I=\frac\pi4$, so $2I=\frac\pi2$ and the second integral $=\frac\pi4\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin x\cos x}{\sin^4x+\cos^4x}dx=\frac\pi4\cdot\frac\pi4=\frac{\pi^2}{16}$.

Q: If $[x]$, $\{x\}$, $[x^2]$ or a min/max of greatest-integer pieces appear
R: "$\int_0^{2.4}[x^2]dx=\alpha+\beta\sqrt2+\gamma\sqrt3+\delta\sqrt5$", "$f=\min\{[x-1],\ldots,[x-10]\}$", "$\int_0^{10}\frac{[x]e^{[x]}}{e^{x-1}}dx$", "$\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{[x]+[\sin x]+4}$".
F: \int_a^b[g(x)]dx=\sum_k k\cdot(\text{length where } [g]=k)
S: Find the points where the inside crosses integers ($x^2=1,2,3,\ldots$ gives $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\ldots$). || On each sub-interval the bracket is constant; write the integral as a sum of constant × length. || For $[x]$ times another function, integrate that function on each unit interval and sum the series.
E: $[x^2]$ on $[0,2.4]$: $(\sqrt2-1)+2(\sqrt3-\sqrt2)+3(2-\sqrt3)+4(\sqrt5-2)+5(2.4-\sqrt5)=9-\sqrt2-\sqrt3-\sqrt5$; sum $6$.

Q: If the limit of a sum $\sum\frac1nf\left(\frac kn\right)$ is in disguise
R: "$\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{n^3}{(n^2+k^2)(n^2+3k^2)}$".
F: \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^nf\left(\frac kn\right)=\int_0^1f(x)dx
S: Divide numerator and denominator by the right power of $n$ so a factor $\frac1n$ is left outside. || Replace $\frac kn$ by $x$ and $\frac1n\sum$ by $\int_0^1$. || Evaluate (partial fractions in $x^2$).
E: $\int_0^1\frac{dx}{(1+x^2)(1+3x^2)}=\int_0^1\left(\frac{3/2}{1+3x^2}-\frac{1/2}{1+x^2}\right)dx=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt3}-\frac\pi8$.
P: 14:2

Q: If $f$ is defined by an integral equation ($f(x)=g(x)+\int_0^x\ldots$ or $+\int_a^b\ldots f(t)dt$)
R: "$f(x)=x+\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos y\,f(y)dy$", "$f(x)=1-2x+\int_0^xe^{x-t}f(t)dt$", "$f(x)+\int_0^x(x-t)f'(t)dt=\ldots$", "$f=\int_0^x(5-|t-3|)dt$ for $x>4$".
F: \int_a^b(\ldots)f(y)dy=k\ \text{(a constant)};\quad\frac d{dx}\int_0^xh(t)dt=h(x)
S: Fixed limits: the integral is a constant $k$; write $f$ with $k$, substitute back to solve for $k$. || Variable limit: differentiate both sides to get a differential equation; use the value at $x=0$. || For $\int_0^x(x-t)g(t)dt$, write it as $x\int g-\int tg$ before differentiating.
E: $f(x)=x+k\sin x$, $k=\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos y(y+k\sin y)dy=\frac\pi2-1+\frac k2$ ⇒ $k=\pi-2$.
P: 14:1,20,27,13

Q: If a limit contains an integral with a variable limit
R: "$\lim_{x\to\pi/2}\frac{\int_{(\pi/2)^3}^{x^3}(\sin(2t^{1/3})+\cos t^{1/3})dt}{(x-\frac\pi2)^2}$".
F: \frac d{dx}\int_a^{g(x)}h(t)dt=h(g(x))\,g'(x)
S: Check it is $\frac00$. || Apply L'Hospital, differentiating the integral with the Leibniz rule. || Repeat if still $\frac00$.
E: After two applications the limit is $\frac{3x^2(2\cos2x-\sin x)}{2}\Big|_{\pi/2}=-\frac{9\pi^2}8$.

Q: If integration by parts relates $\int xF'$, $\int x^2F''$, or $P(x)$ is found from extrema and an integral
R: "$F=xf$, $\int_0^2xF'(x)dx=6$, $\int_0^2x^2F''(x)dx=40$", "cubic $P$ vanishing at $-3$ with min at 1, max at $-1$, $\int_{-1}^1P=18$".
F: \int_0^ax\,F'(x)dx=aF(a)-\int_0^aF(x)dx
S: Integrate by parts to move derivatives off $F$. || Use $F(a)=af(a)$ and the given values. || For polynomials with known extrema, write $P'=k(x-1)(x+1)$, integrate, and use the conditions.
E: $P=k(\frac{x^3}3-x)+6k$; $\int_{-1}^1P=12k=18\Rightarrow k=\frac32$.

Q: If the integrand hides a factorisation ($\frac{t^4+1}{t^6+1}$, $\frac{\sqrt{x(x^2+x+1)}}{\sqrt{x+1}\sqrt{x^4+x^2+1}}$, conjugate surds)
R: "$\int_1^2\frac{t^4+1}{t^6+1}dt$", "$\int_0^2\frac{\sqrt{x(x^2+x+1)}}{\sqrt{x+1}\sqrt{x^4+x^2+1}}dx$", "$4\int_0^1\frac{dx}{\sqrt{3+x^2}+\sqrt{1+x^2}}$".
F: t^6+1=(t^2+1)(t^4-t^2+1),\ \ x^4+x^2+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1)
S: Factor the polynomials and cancel common factors. || Split $\frac{t^4+1}{t^6+1}=\frac1{t^2+1}+\frac{t^2}{t^6+1}$. || Rationalise sums of surds (multiply by the conjugate). || Integrate the simpler pieces.
E: $\left[\tan^{-1}t+\frac13\tan^{-1}t^3\right]_1^2=\tan^{-1}2+\frac13\tan^{-1}8-\frac\pi3$.

Q: If it is a Beta-type integral $\int_0^1x^{m-1}(1-x)^{n-1}dx$
R: "$I(m,n)=\int_0^1x^{m-1}(1-x)^{n-1}dx$; $I(9,14)+I(10,13)$".
F: I(m,n+1)+I(m+1,n)=I(m,n),\quad I(m,n)=\frac{(m-1)!(n-1)!}{(m+n-1)!}
S: Add the integrands: $x^{m-1}(1-x)^{n-1}[(1-x)+x]$. || The sum collapses to $I(m,n)$ with smaller indices.
E: $I(9,14)+I(10,13)=I(9,13)$.

Q: If $\cot^{-1}$ (or $\tan^{-1}$) of a quadratic is integrated
R: "$\int_0^14\cot^{-1}(1-2x+4x^2)dx=a\tan^{-1}2-b\ln5$".
F: \cot^{-1}(1-x(1-x)\ldots):\ \tan^{-1}\frac1{1-2x(1-2x)}=\tan^{-1}2x+\tan^{-1}(1-2x)
S: Write $\cot^{-1}u=\tan^{-1}\frac1u$ and split as $\tan^{-1}a+\tan^{-1}b$ with $a+b=1$, $1-ab=u$. || Use $x\to1-x$ symmetry: $\int_0^1\tan^{-1}(2x-1)dx=0$. || Integrate $\tan^{-1}2x$ by parts.
E: $4\left(\tan^{-1}2-\frac14\ln5\right)=4\tan^{-1}2-\ln5$: $a=4$, $b=1$, $2a+b=9$.
