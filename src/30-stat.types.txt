## If this type comes, solve like this
Q: If mean and variance are given and two observations are unknown
F: x+y=n\bar x-\sum(\text{known}),\qquad x^2+y^2=n(\sigma^2+\bar x^2)-\sum(\text{known}^2)
N: Then $xy=\frac{(x+y)^2-(x^2+y^2)}2$ — solve the quadratic, or answer the asked symmetric expression directly.
P: 06:1,5,6,7,14,17,34,36,42,9

Q: If an observation was wrong and corrected
F: \sum x_{\text{new}}=\sum x-\text{wrong}+\text{right},\quad\sum x^2_{\text{new}}=\sum x^2-\text{wrong}^2+\text{right}^2
N: Get $\sum x^2$ from $n(\sigma^2+\bar x^2)$ first.
P: 06:3,15,16,38,8,41,39,59

Q: If sums like $\sum(x_i-a)$, $\sum(x_i-a)^2$ or $\sum(x_i+a)^2$ are given
F: \sigma^2=\frac{\sum(x_i-a)^2}n-\left(\frac{\sum(x_i-a)}n\right)^2
N: Two such sums give two equations in $\sum x$ and $\sum x^2$; subtract to eliminate.
P: 06:2,20,21,22,25

Q: If two groups are combined
F: \sigma^2=\frac{n_1(\sigma_1^2+d_1^2)+n_2(\sigma_2^2+d_2^2)}{n_1+n_2},\ \ d_i=\bar x_i-\bar x
N: Equivalent and often faster: add $\sum x^2=n(\sigma^2+\bar x^2)$ of each group.
P: 06:24,53,54,55,56,19,40

Q: If the data is an AP, a linear transform, or $1,\ldots,n$
F: \text{variance of AP}=d^2\frac{n^2-1}{12};\qquad y=ax+b:\ \sigma_y^2=a^2\sigma_x^2
P: 06:4,13,30,31,26,27,29,28,50,37

Q: If it asks mean deviation (about mean or median)
F: \text{MD}=\frac1n\sum|x_i-c|
N: Sort the data, find the median, sum absolute deviations. For $k,2k,\ldots,1000k$: pair from both ends.
P: 06:10,17,18,42,43,44,45,46,47

Q: If it is a frequency table with unknown frequencies
F: \bar x=\frac{\sum f_ix_i}{\sum f_i},\quad\sigma^2=\frac{\sum f_ix_i^2}{\sum f_i}-\bar x^2
P: 06:11,12,23,49,48,10

Q: If frequencies are binomial coefficients ${}^nC_r$
F: \sum{}^nC_r=2^n,\quad\sum r\,{}^nC_r=n2^{n-1},\quad\sum{}^nC_ra^r=(1+a)^n
P: 06:51,52

Q: If data is a GP or a mix with other chapters
F: \text{use GP sums for } \sum x \text{ and } \sum x^2\ (\text{ratio } r^2)
P: 06:32,33,57,58,35,60
