## If this type comes, solve like this
Q: If the mean and variance are given and two observations are unknown
R: "Mean of five observations is 5, variance 9.2; three are 1, 3, 8", "mean and variance of $2,4,10,x,12,14,y$ are 8 and 16", "mean 6.5 and variance 10.25 for 6 distinct observations".
F: \sum x_i=n\bar x,\qquad\sum x_i^2=n(\sigma^2+\bar x^2)
S: From the mean: the unknown sum $x+y=n\bar x-(\text{known sum})$. || From the variance: $x^2+y^2=n(\sigma^2+\bar x^2)-(\text{known squares})$. || Then $xy=\frac{(x+y)^2-(x^2+y^2)}2$; $x,y$ are roots of $t^2-(x+y)t+xy=0$. || Use the extra condition ($x>y$, distinct) to pick the order.
E: $x+y=13$, $x^2+y^2=171-74=97$ ⇒ $xy=36$ ⇒ $4$ and $9$, ratio $4:9$.
H: Trap: using $\sigma^2=\frac{\sum x^2}n$ without subtracting $\bar x^2$.

Q: If an observation was recorded wrongly and is corrected (or a value is removed/added)
R: "Observation 9 should be 11", "two observations taken as 9 and 10 instead of 7 and 14", "a teacher of 60 retires and a new one joins", "add one more observation to 5 data".
F: \text{correct } \sum x=\text{old }\sum x-\text{wrong}+\text{right};\ \ \text{same for }\sum x^2
S: Recover $\sum x$ and $\sum x^2$ from the old mean and variance. || Replace the wrong values by the right ones in both sums. || Recompute: new mean $=\frac{\sum x}n$, new variance $=\frac{\sum x^2}n-\bar x^2$. || If the count changes, divide by the new $n$.
E: $\sum x=200$, $\sum x^2=2080$; after correction $202$ and $2120$ ⇒ mean $10.1$, variance $106-102.01=3.99$.
P: 06:3,15,16,38,8,41,39,59

Q: If sums like $\sum(x_i-a)$, $\sum(x_i-a)^2$ or $\sum(x_i+a)^2$ are given
R: "$\sum(x_i+2)^2=180$, $\sum(x_i-1)^2=90$; SD", "$\sum(x_i-2)=30$, $\sum(x_i-\beta)^2=98$", "mean and SD both 16; mean of $(x_i-4)^2$".
F: \sigma^2=\frac{\sum(x_i-a)^2}n-\left(\frac{\sum(x_i-a)}n\right)^2\ \ (\text{variance is unchanged by shifts})
S: Subtract the two squared sums to get $\sum x_i$ (the squares cancel). || Get $\sum x_i^2$ from either sum. || Variance $=\frac{\sum x^2}n-\bar x^2$. || For the mean of $(x_i-a)^2$: $\sigma^2+(\bar x-a)^2$.
E: Difference: $6\sum x+30=90\Rightarrow\sum x=10$; then $\sum x^2=100$, $\sigma^2=10-1=9$, SD $=3$.
P: 06:2,20,21,22,25

Q: If two groups are combined (or one group is found from the whole)
R: "4 observations with mean 1, variance 13 and 6 with mean 2, variance 1; combined variance", "first sample of 100 with mean 15, SD 3; whole group of 250…", "sets A, B with 5 elements each".
F: \bar x=\frac{n_1\bar x_1+n_2\bar x_2}{n_1+n_2},\quad\sigma^2=\frac{n_1(\sigma_1^2+d_1^2)+n_2(\sigma_2^2+d_2^2)}{n_1+n_2},\ d_i=\bar x_i-\bar x
S: Compute the combined mean. || Either use the formula with $d_i$, or add the $\sum x^2$ of both groups: $\sum x^2=n(\sigma^2+\bar x^2)$. || Combined variance $=\frac{\text{total }\sum x^2}{N}-\bar x^2$. || To find a missing group, run the same equations backwards.
E: $\sum x^2=56+30=86$, $\bar x=1.6$ ⇒ $\sigma^2=8.6-2.56=6.04$.
P: 06:24,53,54,55,56,19,40

Q: If the data is an AP or the first $n$ natural/even numbers
R: "Variance of an increasing AP $b_1,\ldots,b_{11}$ is 90", "variance of $8,21,34,\ldots,320$", "variance of the first $n$ natural numbers is 10", "$y_i=i(x_i-i)$ for an AP", "mean of $7\times8,10\times10,\ldots$".
F: \text{variance of }1,2,\ldots,n=\frac{n^2-1}{12};\ \ \text{AP with } n \text{ terms, difference } d:\ \frac{d^2(n^2-1)}{12}
S: Identify $n$ and $d$. || Use the formula directly. || For products/expressions of AP terms, write the general term and use $\sum k$, $\sum k^2$.
E: $\frac{d^2\cdot120}{12}=90\Rightarrow d=3$. And $\frac{n^2-1}{12}=10\Rightarrow n=11$; $\frac{4(m^2-1)}{12}=16\Rightarrow m=7$.
P: 06:4,30,31,26,27

Q: If the data is linearly transformed ($y=ax+b$), or has a symmetric pattern ($\pm a$, $\frac12\pm d$)
R: "$Y=\{ax+b:x\in X\}$ with mean 30 and variance 750", "half the observations are $a$, half $-a$; add $b$", "$b=a+c$; SD of $a+2,b+2,c+2$", "variance of $3,7,12,a,43-a$ is a natural number".
F: \overline{ax+b}=a\bar x+b,\quad\sigma^2_{ax+b}=a^2\sigma_x^2
S: Find the mean and variance of the base data. || Apply the transformation rules (shifts do not change variance). || Solve for $a$, $b$.
E: $X=1..19$: mean 10, variance 30 ⇒ $30a^2=750$, $a=\pm5$, $10a+b=30$.

Q: If the mean deviation (about the mean or the median) is asked
R: "Mean deviation about the median of $k,2k,\ldots,1000k$ is 500", "MD about the median of $3,5,7,2k,12,16,21,24$ is 6", "MD of $a,b,8,5,10$ with mean 6".
F: \text{MD}(a)=\frac{\sum|x_i-a|}n\ \ (\text{or }\frac{\sum f_i|x_i-a|}{\sum f_i})
S: Find the central value (mean, or median — middle term after sorting). || Compute $|x_i-a|$ for each observation (with frequencies if given). || Average them. || With unknowns, find them from mean/variance first.
E: $k,\ldots,1000k$: median $500.5k$, $\sum|x_i-500.5k|=k\cdot250000$, MD $=250k=500\Rightarrow k=2$, $k^2=4$.

Q: If a frequency table has unknown frequencies or an unknown value
R: "Mean and variance of the data are $\mu$ and 19; find $\lambda+\mu$", "frequency distribution of marks of 20 students with $x$", "median of grouped data".
F: \bar x=\frac{\sum f_ix_i}{\sum f_i},\ \ \sigma^2=\frac{\sum f_ix_i^2}{\sum f_i}-\bar x^2,\ \ \text{median}=l+\frac{\frac N2-C}{f}h
S: Use $\sum f_i=N$ for one equation. || Mean (and variance) give further equations. || Solve for the unknown frequency or value. || For grouped medians, find the median class first.
P: 06:11,12,23,49,48

Q: If the frequencies are binomial coefficients $\binom nr$
R: "Values $0,2,4,\ldots,2^n$ with frequencies $\binom n0,\ldots,\binom nn$", "values $r(r-1)$ with frequencies $\binom nr$".
F: \sum\binom nr2^r=3^n,\ \ \sum r\binom nr=n2^{n-1},\ \ \sum r(r-1)\binom nr=n(n-1)2^{n-2}
S: Total frequency $=2^n$. || Write $\sum f_ix_i$ with a binomial identity. || Mean $=\frac{\sum f_ix_i}{2^n}$; set it equal to the given value.
E: Values $r(r-1)$: mean $=\frac{n(n-1)2^{n-2}}{2^n}=\frac{n(n-1)}4$.
P: 06:51,52

Q: If the data is a GP, or statistics is mixed with another chapter (systems of equations, bounds)
R: "$a_1,\ldots,a_5$ in GP with mean $\frac{31}{10}$", "$x_i$ in GP with $x_1=3$, ratio $\frac12$; new data", "variance of $1,1,\ldots,1,k$ less than 10", "SD of $-1,0,1,k$ is $\sqrt5$".
F: \text{GP sums}: a\frac{r^n-1}{r-1};\ \ \text{squares of a GP form a GP with ratio } r^2
S: Write the terms in $a$ and $r$; use the mean to fix them (try simple ratios like 2 or $\frac12$). || Sum the squares as a GP. || For inequalities, write the variance as a function of $k$ and solve.
E: $1,0,-1,k$: $\frac{2+k^2}4-\frac{k^2}{16}=5$ ⇒ $3k^2=72$, $k=2\sqrt6$.
P: 06:32,33,57,58,35,60
